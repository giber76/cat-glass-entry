# Five Seconds to Explain the Glass

The single sentence a house cat would say in the five seconds after sweeping a glass off the table — at most 200 characters, in the cat's own voice. No apology and no confession, just the most convincing case a cat can make for itself.

## The sentence (111 characters)

> That glass was squatting in my sunbeam, empty and staring at me, so I evicted it — the rent was overdue anyway.

## Why this line

- **Cat logic, not human logic:** the glass was squatting in *my* sunbeam — a territorial dispute. It was empty (worthless) and staring at me (provocation). The cat is not sorry; the cat is the landlord.
- **Fresh idea:** an eviction, not gravity, not a slippery edge, not "it was already falling". The cat-as-landlord is a comic idea the judge has not seen ten thousand times.
- **Fits five seconds:** one spoken line a cat could plausibly deliver, not a paragraph of reasoning.
- **Punch last:** "the rent was overdue anyway" lands in the final words — the eviction was overdue, and the cat did the household a favour.
- **No padding:** every clause earns its place — squatting, empty, staring, evicted, rent overdue. Nothing to apologise for, nothing to confess: the glass was a bad tenant.
